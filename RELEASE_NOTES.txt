1. AQuA KYC rewritten from "proposed concept" to the product as built
   - aqua/kyc.html: full rewrite, then simplified for a non-technical first reader.
     Hero, a before/after picture (three separate connections vs one through AQuA),
     "What your staff can do" (four one-line cards), "Three things your auditors will
     ask about", "Where it stands", "Going live". All technical detail is collapsed
     into a "For your technology team" section at the bottom.
   - aqua/index.html and aqua/demos.html rewritten in the same plain voice.
   - index.html: the KYC console scenario is now the real lookup flow - verification
     hub, registry search returning two records (one active, one inactive duplicate),
     consent and release, compliance and audit. Tab is "KYC lookup".
   - index.html: NEW third console scenario, "New KYC record - possible duplicate".
     Five steps: record intake, the checks that run before anything is sent, filing
     with the registry, the registry answering PROBABLE MATCH with two candidates,
     and the decision trail. Ends on "Is this the same person?" with a human choice.
     Added because the console previously showed only lookup and synchronisation,
     which understated the product.
   - index.html: the three console tabs are now named after the product capabilities -
     "Search & download", "Create a record", "Update & sync" - so a visitor can map
     what they are watching to what the product does. Download and update were both
     already in there, buried inside steps with other names.
   - index.html: the console footer strip said "Read-only" and "No bank write access".
     That was written for AQuA Payments and is wrong for KYC, where filing a record
     and updating an address are writes. Now reads: synthetic data, filed under your
     own registration, nothing written without approval, human sign-off before any action.
   - index.html: the KYC synchronization scenario kept its six steps but lost the
     "conceptual / illustrative only / not a real integration" hedging. Still states
     synthetic data, because that is true.

   Claim position: headline treats AQuA KYC as a real product. Search, record retrieval
   with OTP consent and record creation are stated as built and tested end to end;
   face/signed-form consent, periodic update and synchronisation as in build. "Tested"
   is defined on the page as against a CKYCRR 2.0 simulator built from CERSAI's API
   guide v2.1, explicitly not a substitute for CERSAI's own testbed. The site never
   claims CERSAI certification, and says CERSAI registers Reporting Entities rather
   than technology vendors.
