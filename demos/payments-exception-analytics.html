Industry case · Financial services · Interactive demo

From transaction noise to fee income and retained customers.

A large retail bank, about nine billion payments a year across UPI, cards, IMPS, NEFT and RTGS, analysed for exception load, fraud pressure and reconciliation health, then converted into five costed moves.

Headline insightUPI is 71% of transaction count but 22% of value; RTGS is 0.3% of count and 41% of value. Queues worked by alert age instead of value-at-risk leave the highest-value channel with the slowest resolution, and the fraud data shows the same inversion: the rarest typology converts to real fraud at nearly three times the rate of the noisiest one.

Insight. Three hotspots, three causes: UPI 18–24h (evening consumer peak, beneficiary-bank timeouts), NEFT 09–15h (batch congestion), RTGS 15–18h (cut-off pressure). One "exception team" is actually three problems. → Retry orchestration for UPI (Move 4), batch smoothing for NEFT (Move 5), a value desk for RTGS (Move 2).

The failed-payment funnel: where value leaks

Average day: what happens to the ~88k transactions that fail on first attempt, thousands

Insight. ~9k payments a day end the day unresolved, and an estimated 2.4k customers sit in the churn-risk band (two or more failures in 30 days). Failed payments are a revenue problem: lost fee income today, lost customers tomorrow. → Smart retry (Move 4) attacks the top of this funnel, where recovery is cheapest.

3 · The strategyFive moves, process changes before builds

Click a move to include or exclude it from the programme
Guardrail · GovernanceAutomation with audit trails and human sign-off on valueEvery auto-repair and retry is logged, reversible and capped; anything above the value threshold routes to a person. Model and rule performance is reviewed monthly against false-positive load and customer complaints. How: the same discipline the June rollout used: expand coverage, never relax the controls.
